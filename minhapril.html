Task Response 4 The task asks the writer to discuss two specific views — that education can solve the problem of junk food overconsumption, and that education cannot — and then give a personal opinion. This essay fundamentally misunderstands the task. The first body paragraph argues that fast food is useful, cheap, and varied, which is not the same as arguing that education cannot solve the problem. The second body paragraph comes closer to addressing the "education can help" side, but mixes in an unrelated point about home cooking being cheaper. The conclusion does not clearly state or defend a position on the education debate. The personal example of McDonald's and KFC serving food in "one minute and thirty seconds" is irrelevant to the argument. All in all, large portions of the essay are off-topic, which significantly limits the Task Response score. Read Bình’s essays to learn how to properly develop relevant ideas for this task.
Coherence & Cohesion 4 There is a visible attempt at structure — two body paragraphs and a conclusion — and sequencing devices like "First of all," "Secondly," "Thirdly," and "On the other hand" are used. However, cohesion within paragraphs breaks down repeatedly. Sentences are often fragments or loosely connected: "Because is make faster and smaller, easy to carry" has no subject and no clear logical link to what preceded it. "Thirdly, fast food have multiple choice. In addition, people can choose like burger and french fries..." runs ideas together without developing them. Overall progression is difficult to follow.
Lexical resource 3-4 Vocabulary is very limited and frequently inaccurate. "Casual food" is used where "home-cooked food" or "regular meals" is meant. "Hand made food" is unnatural, "home-cooked meals" is the standard phrase. "Make a lines" (stand in a line), "cooker" (cook/chef), "a burger have two ingredients is meat and vegetables" — all reflect very limited control of lexical items. "To much" (too much) in the introduction is a basic spelling error. The range does not permit meaningful variation, and the same ideas are expressed in very simple, repetitive terms throughout. There is also apparent confusion between "fast food" and "junk food," which are related but not identical, and the task uses "junk food" specifically.
Grammatical range & accuracy 3-4 Grammatical errors are very frequent and often impede meaning. Key issues include missing subjects ("Because is make faster"), incorrect verb forms (“fast food have," "can be solve"), fragments (Because is make faster and smaller, easy to carry), sentence structure breakdown (“the disadvantages of junk food and can lead to obesity”, “put in a food box to carry and can be decorated”). Simple sentences occasionally come through correctly, but complex structures are almost always faulty. The volume of errors at the grammatical level is the most significant barrier to a higher score.

Key areas to improve:
  • Re-read the task question carefully before writing — the essay must address whether education is the solution, not whether fast food is good or bad. Every paragraph should connect back to that central debate. Work on sentence-level basics: every sentence needs a subject and a verb ("Because is make" → "Because it is made quickly"). Practice using articles (a, an, the) — they are missing in almost every sentence. Distinguish between "fast food" (speed of preparation) and "junk food" (nutritional quality) — the task is specifically about junk food and health. Avoid one-sentence "paragraphs" disguised as reasons — each point needs at minimum two to three sentences of explanation.
