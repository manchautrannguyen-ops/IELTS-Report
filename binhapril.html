Task

Task: Scientists agree that many people eat too much junk food and it is damaging their health. Some people think that this problem can be solved by education, while others believe that this education will not work. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

Essay

Some individuals believe that education can help people not overeat junk food to protect their health, while others suggest that this education will not work efficiently. In my opinion, education can tackle this issue in some cases, because it completely depends on the choices of people receiving that education.

On the one hand, education plays an important role in preventing the increase of junk food consumption. Firstly, it is good for children to receive education about these issues when they are in school. When children are taught about the drawbacks of overeating junk food, they can gain deep understanding to know what is good for their health. For example, lessons in schools including knowledge about the properties of junk food or the negative side of eating too much that kind of food can raise awareness of children. As a result, children know the proper amount of junk food they can take in. Secondly, governments should organize some awareness campaigns for adults to raise their awareness. As some adults are too busy at 9-5 work, they would like to eat junk food to save time on cooking or if they have kids, they will allow their children to eat junk food instead of preparing home made dishes for their kids. Therefore, when there are some awareness campaigns about overeating junk food and spending more time on cooking will enhance their mental health, adults can expand their knowledge and pay more attention to their diet. After that, they can affect their children by teaching and cooking foods for their kids.

On the other hand, receiving proper education is a part of addressing this problem, because it depends on the choices. Gaining enough knowledge about the consequences of eating too much junk food does not mean that they can control their consumption of that food. Because, there are no rules about eating junk food, so they can eat that food freely as they want. In some cases when people feel stressed, they will tend to overeat junk food. For these reasons, education is just a tool helping them to be aware about their choice of eating junk food, people will be the final person to determine whether to eat or not. Đoạn này chưa được phát triển đầy đủ. Phía ủng hộ giáo dục đưa ra hai luận điểm rõ ràng, có ví dụ minh họa, trong khi phía phản biện gần như chỉ xoay quanh một ý. Nên bổ sung một lý do riêng biệt thứ hai để giải thích vì sao giáo dục không hiệu quả.(This paragraph is under-developed. The pro-education side gets two fully developed points with examples, while the counter-argument side gets essentially one idea restated multiple times. You should add a second distinct reason why education fails.)

In conclusion, education is needed to help people enhance their awareness about the consequences of eating too much junk food. However, people still hold the power on choosing what they want to eat and how much they want to take in that kind of food.

CriterionScoreComment
Task Response 6.5 The essay addresses both views and presents a personal opinion. The position — that education helps but ultimately depends on individual choice — is original and coherent. Both body paragraphs contain relevant ideas that are generally extended. However, there are weaknesses preventing Band 7: the "against education" argument is underdeveloped compared to the "for education" side, which gets two well-elaborated points while the opposing view relies mainly on one central idea (personal choice) repeated in different ways. The opinion is also somewhat hedged and cautious rather than clearly defended. Read Huy’s essay, which scored 7 in Task Response, to see how to develop better ideas for this task.
Coherence & Cohesion 6.5 The essay is generally well-organised with a clear overall progression. Paragraph structure is logical and the writer uses a good range of cohesive devices: firstly, secondly, as a result, therefore, however, after that, for these reasons. However, some issues limit the score. "After that" in the first body paragraph is an odd connector — it implies a temporal sequence where a logical consequence is intended (as a result or in turn would be better). The second body paragraph opens with "receiving proper education is a part of addressing this problem" — this reads more like agreement than a counterargument, which blurs the paragraph's purpose. The sentence beginning "Because, there are no rules..." is a fragment, not a full sentence.
Lexical resource 6.5 There is a reasonable range of vocabulary used with generally appropriate precision: drawbacks, overeating, raise awareness, consequences, enhance their mental health, awareness campaigns. The writer attempts some less common phrasing and largely succeeds. However, repetition is a notable weakness — junk food appears roughly fifteen times without meaningful variation, and awareness is overused across both body paragraphs. Some word choices are imprecise or unnatural: "take in" (consume would be more formal and appropriate), "the final person to determine" (the one who ultimately decides), "that kind of food" appears repeatedly as an awkward substitute for a better synonym. Spelling appears clean throughout, which is a strength.
Grammatical range & accuracy 6.5 The essay demonstrates a mix of simple and complex structures, with complex sentences generally handled well. There is evidence of subordinate clauses, conditionals, and relative clauses used with reasonable accuracy. Errors exist but are mostly minor and do not impede communication: "know what is good for their health" is slightly redundant following "deep understanding," "spending more time on cooking will enhance their mental health" is a debatable and grammatically awkward claim, "people will be the final person" shows number disagreement (people...person). The fragment sentence ("Because, there are no rules...") is the most notable grammatical lapse. Overall, error-free sentences are frequent enough to suggest solid grammatical control at this level.
Overall estimated band6.5
What Would Push This to Band 7
  • Balance the two sides more equally. The pro-education side gets two fully developed points with examples; the counter-argument side gets essentially one idea restated multiple times. A second distinct reason why education fails, such as the influence of food marketing, price and convenience barriers, or psychological factors like addiction, would significantly strengthen Task Response.
  • Reduce repetition of key terms. Replacing repeated instances of junk food with processed food, fast food, unhealthy food options and replacing repeated awareness with understanding, consciousness, knowledge would lift the Lexical Resource score noticeably.
  • Fix the sentence fragment. "Because, there are no rules..." must be joined to the preceding sentence. In academic writing, because should not begin a standalone sentence.
  • Sharpen the personal opinion. The position is interesting but somewhat passive — the essay tells us education depends on choices without arguing forcefully for what should therefore be done. A clearer stance, even one sentence defending a specific recommendation, would make the opinion more convincing.

This candidate demonstrates genuine structural variety: past continuous, conditionals, modals, subordinate clauses. She also shows a slightly richer discourse marker repertoire ("besides," "fortunately," "moreover"), even if those markers aren't always used precisely.

The main ceiling is lexical resource. Getting from 5.5 to 6.0+ here would require consciously using more varied and precise word choices rather than defaulting to the first word that comes to mind.

Priority areas to push toward Band 6.0+:
  • Fix the "so that" overuse: it's appearing where "so," "because," or "in order to" would be correct. Misused connectives actively hurt the coherence score
  • Tense consistency in narratives: the Part 2 answer drifts between past and present; committing to past tense throughout would immediately improve grammatical accuracy.
  • Build a bank of less-common vocabulary around IELTS topic areas: even one or two precise words per topic (e.g.: deteriorate, fumble) signals lexical awareness to examiners
