Task Response 3–4 The task asks the writer to state and defend a position on whether handheld entertainment is harmful. A very loose position is implied — that games are not harmful if used correctly — but it is never clearly stated in the introduction and has to be inferred from scattered personal commentary. The essay is heavily weighted toward personal anecdote ("like me, all my friend I have now," "it make me have a lot memory I can't forget when I was a child") rather than developed arguments. The second body paragraph appears to cut off mid-sentence ("can make your braint"), meaning the essay is incomplete. The task requires engagement with both the harmful and non-harmful dimensions with reasoned support; what is provided is largely personal reflection without argumentative structure. The point about streamers and YouTubers earning money is relevant but undeveloped. Overall, the prompt is only minimally and partially addressed.
Coherence & Cohesion 3–4 There is an attempt at a two-paragraph structure using "First" and "Second," but the body paragraphs are not focused on single ideas — they wander across multiple loosely connected thoughts without clear progression. The first body paragraph moves from mood improvement, to making friends, to the writer's own childhood memories, to earning money from gaming, all without clear links between them. Sentences are frequently run-on or fragmented. "Like all think we do, both of them will be harmful if we use in a wrong way" is difficult to parse as a coherent sentence. The essay ends abruptly and incompletely, which severely limits coherence. Reference and substitution are inconsistent — "that devices," "them," and "it" are used without always making clear what they refer to.
Lexical resource 3–4 Vocabulary is very limited and frequently inaccurate. Errors include: "Specially" (especially), "beatiful" (beautiful), "though" used where "thoughts" is meant, "connecting" used where "connected" is needed, "a change to know" (a chance to know), "each others" (each other), "braint" (brain — and the sentence is unfinished), "scare to talk" (afraid to talk / scared of talking), "in time" used where "in moderation" is meant, wrong collocation ("pay a lot of time") and wrong word form ("connect with social", "a harmful of play video game"). The range does not extend beyond basic everyday vocabulary, and the same ideas are expressed repeatedly in very simple terms. These errors are frequent and often impede meaning, placing this dimension at Band 3–4.
Grammatical range & accuracy 3 Grammatical errors are pervasive and frequently prevent meaning from coming through clearly. Key issues include: missing articles throughout ("a video games," "video game on handheld devices," "a devices"); subject-verb disagreement ("video game... are," "it make me," "everything have"); incorrect pronoun forms ("make we develop yourself," "when you don't have any energy, that time you will stop"); missing verbs ("It will a devices help you", "you difficult to stop"); incorrect verb forms ("I connecting by play game," "I don't scare to talk"); singular-plural ("make friend", "all my friend", "play game", "bad and good thing"), preposition ("a lot them"), incorrect verb forms ("by play game", "a harmful of play video game"), auxiliary verbs ("help me more confident") and run-on sentences that chain clauses without punctuation or connectors. Simple sentences are attempted but most contain at least one error. The essay is also incomplete, which further limits the evidence available of grammatical control. This profile aligns with Band 3 on the rubric.

Key areas to improve
  • Write a clear introductory sentence that states your position directly, without personal stories. Keep each paragraph to one main idea and develop it with explanation and a general example — avoid writing about your own personal life.
  • Learn the basic rule that every English sentence needs a subject and a verb, and practise identifying when both are present.
  • Work on article use (a, an, the) — nearly every noun phrase in this essay is missing an article.
  • Proofread before finishing — the essay cuts off mid-sentence, which immediately signals to an examiner that the response is incomplete.
  • This essay reads as though the writer has things to say but hasn't yet learned how to express them in academic English. The most immediate priorities are finishing the essay fully, replacing personal anecdotes with general arguments, and working on article use and subject-verb agreement at the sentence level.
